# Project Exploration: 

## Project Title:

https://github.com/wesnoth/wesnoth

## Project Homepage: 
https://www.wesnoth.org/

## Product: 
Wesnoth is a turn based strategy game with fantasy themes. It has single player, online multiplayer, and local multiplayer modes. There are a variety of different campaigns that the player can choose from. The campaigns have a lot of lore and story, and there are a lot of combat elements in the game as well where the player must try to survive and defeat enemies.

## Target User Community: 
The target user community would be people who enjoy fantasy games, strategy games, combat games, games heavy in story, and turn based games. Wesnoth mainly serves the purpose of entertainment, it is a video game, however it falls under all the categories listed and would satisfy people looking to play any of those types of games.

## Getting Started: 
I would say that their website is a little hard to navigate, with many different links for developers. They have a getting started but it appears to just explain how the game works. However, I have found some helpful guides for doing code on their project, such as this one: https://wiki.wesnoth.org/HackingWesnoth, which explains some coding guidelines for C++ that they want their developers to follow. I would say overall, its a little bit challenging to figure out which of the many links will have the information you are looking for. 

https://wiki.wesnoth.org/Project is the link to the page that contains many different links for developers to follow.

## Welcoming to Newcomers: 
They have a GettingStarted page, but it was not what I was expecting. It just explained how to use the game. They have a Discord which they encourage new developers to use. There is a GoodFirstIssue tag but it seems to inconsistently get used. They had some issues created with it in January and then before that, the most recent was February 2025. I found a comment thread under one issue showing engagement between another developer and one who worked on a GoodFirstIssue labeled issue. So, this shows that their community does engage and support developers.
